Chapter 01

There is no Colorado painting license.

Which means the paperwork that protects you is the paperwork you ask for. Here is the list.

Short answer: no. Colorado has no statewide license for painting contractors. Some cities license or register contractors, and requirements can depend on whether the job needs a permit. For a typical repaint, what protects you is insurance, a written warranty, lien waivers and real references.

The state answer

A national licensing reference that tracks this state by state puts it bluntly: Colorado has no statewide painting-contractor credential. Local licensing programs, including Denver's, Pikes Peak Regional's, Aurora's and Fort Collins', do not list a separate painting class; whether you need anything depends on the city and on whether the work requires a permit.

Denver's own contractor licensing page is where to start for work inside city limits. Its licenses are tied to supervisor certificates, and the city does not offer reciprocity with other jurisdictions.

What "licensed" usually means on a painter's truck

  • A city business license or registration in a town where they work.
  • A sales and use tax license, which is a tax account, not a skills test.
  • A contractor license from a city for work that needs permits.
  • Sometimes, nothing in particular. Ask.

What actually protects you

1. Insurance you can confirm

Ask for a certificate of general liability insurance sent straight from the insurer or agent, with your name as certificate holder. A photo of a certificate from last year is not the same thing. Ask whether the people working at your house are covered by workers' compensation.

2. A warranty in writing

A spoken "we stand behind our work" is worth what you paid for it. Read how to read a painting warranty before you compare them.

3. Lien waivers with each payment

Colorado's mechanics' lien law lets unpaid contractors, subcontractors and suppliers file liens on the property they improved. A lien waiver collected with each payment is your receipt that those amounts are settled. More in what a painting contract should include.

4. References you actually call

Ask for two or three jobs from the last year, ideally similar to yours. Ask the homeowner what went wrong and how it was handled. Every job has a hiccup; the answer tells you about the painter.

5. Lead-safe certification for older homes

If your home was built before 1978, the federal EPA Renovation, Repair and Painting rule applies to work that disturbs painted surfaces. Ask for the firm's certification. Our Colorado rules page covers what changes on the job.

Red flags

  • A large cash deposit before any materials are bought or dates are set.
  • A one-line quote: "Paint house, $X."
  • No product named, or "we'll use whatever's good."
  • Insurance "in the truck" but never sent.
  • Pressure to sign today.

Voice answer: how do I check a painter in Colorado?

Ask for an insurance certificate sent from the insurer, a written itemized estimate, the warranty in writing, and recent references you call yourself. For pre-1978 homes, ask for EPA lead-safe certification.

Homeowner questions

Do painters need a license in Colorado?

Not from the state. Colorado has no statewide painting-contractor credential. Local programs vary by city and by whether the job needs a permit, so check with your city if the work involves permits.

What does it mean when a Colorado painter says they are licensed?

It can mean a city business license, a city contractor registration, a sales tax license, or nothing specific. Ask which one, and ask to see it.

What insurance should a house painter carry?

At minimum, general liability insurance that covers damage to your property. Ask for a certificate sent directly by the insurer or agent, and ask whether the workers on your job are covered by workers' compensation.

What is a lien waiver?

A signed document in which a contractor or supplier gives up lien rights for amounts already paid. Collecting one with each payment helps protect you from a lien if the painter does not pay their own suppliers or crew.
